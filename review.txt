Reviewer Comments:

Reviewer: 1

Comments:
Summary:
This letter proposes a single-step speech enhancement system derived from a NCSN++ backbone. The method replaces the multi-step flow-matching inference process with residual prediction, energy-adaptive conditioning, and a hybrid perceptual loss, reporting substantially reduced inference latency on VoiceBank-DEMAND while remaining close to the authors' 5-step FlowSE reference.

Major Comment:
A major concern is that the proposed single-step formulation appears to substantially change the generative nature of the original flow-matching model. By replacing the stochastic ODE state with a deterministic zero-state input and training the network as a residual mapper, the system seems closer to a deterministic supervised SE model than to an accelerated flow-matching sampler.

This distinction is central to the paper's claim. The manuscript frames the contribution as reducing the NFE of a flow-matching generative SE model. However, it is not clear that the proposed method accelerates the original flow-matching inference process; rather, it appears to replace it with a deterministic estimator using an NCSN++ backbone. The contribution may therefore need to be positioned and evaluated as a deterministic regression-based SE system, rather than as a true 1-NFE flow-matching model.

This positioning also affects the experimental evaluation. On VoiceBank-DEMAND, strong deterministic/discriminative SE systems currently achieve very competitive, and often superior, objective scores compared with generative SE models [1], [2]. For example, MP-SENet reports PESQ 3.50 [3], and SEMamba reports PESQ 3.55, or 3.69 with PCS [4], whereas the proposed method reports PESQ 3.062. Therefore, if the intended contribution is the residual parameterization and energy-adaptive conditioning, these components should be validated in a deterministic SE setting against substantially stronger one-pass baselines, such as MP-SENet, SEMamba, and/or related recent supervised regressors. Although the authors emphasize closeness to the 5-step FlowSE reference rather than cross-paper ranking, restricting the evaluation mainly to FlowSE-family baselines is not sufficient once the proposed method no longer clearly retains the generative flow-matching inference process. Without these comparisons, the reported speedup mainly shows that a one-pass deterministic residual network is faster than a multi-step generative solver, which is expected and leaves the novelty/significance of the contribution unclear.

Minor Comment 1:
The motivation for zero-state initialization should be clarified. The authors state that they use a four-channel input with zero tensors in order to avoid modifying the input convolutional layers and structurally altering the network capacity. However, changing the original four-channel input to a two-channel noisy-speech input would only affect the parameters of the first convolutional layer, which is negligible compared with the full NCSN++ backbone, likely much smaller than 1% of the total parameter count. It would be helpful to provide an ablation comparing the current four-channel input with zero-state initialization against a simpler two-channel input model that directly consumes the noisy spectrogram, since otherwise it is unclear whether zero-state initialization contributes beyond preserving architectural similarity to FlowSE.

Minor Comment 2:
The effectiveness of the proposed method over the naive FlowSE (N=1) baseline appears somewhat metric-dependent. While the proposed method improves PESQ, FlowSE (N=1) achieves better SI-SDR, and its ESTOI, BAK, and OVRL scores are comparable to or slightly better than those of the proposed method. This suggests that the benefits of the proposed residual parameterization and energy-adaptive conditioning are not uniform across metrics.

Reviewer: 2

Comments:


The paper takes an NCSN++ backbone (used in FlowSE and other speech enhancement works), zeroes out the flow state input, adds an energy-derived FiLM scalar, and trains it as a single-step model using a standard hybrid spectral/waveform loss. The authors frame this as "single-step flow matching," but that is misleading. Once you remove the flow state and the ODE, there is no flow matching happening here. This is simply a standard discriminative regressor that reuses a popular diffusion/flow backbone.

Discriminative models using the exact same NCSN++ backbone are already known to match or exceed generative models on speech enhancement tasks (especially on SI-SDR), a fact established in prior literature that this paper fails to cite or discuss. Because the method is neither flow matching nor methodologically novel, the paper is not suitable for publication in this venue.

Major points:

1. Mislabeled Method & Overstated Novelty:
The paper repeatedly claims to propose a "single-step flow matching" model. This is incorrect. By setting $x_t$ to zero, eliminating the velocity field/ODE solver entirely, and training via direct reconstruction loss on $(y, x_0)$ pairs, the flow matching math in Section II is completely unused in Section III. Reusing an architecture previously trained with flow matching does not make a direct regressor a flow-matching method. The title, abstract, and manuscript must stop framing a standard discriminative baseline as a novel flow-matching variant.
The only minor architectural modification here is the Energy-Adaptive Conditioning, which is far too incremental to justify a full publication.

2. Omission of Key Prior Work:
The paper presents the idea of training an NCSN++ backbone discriminatively as if it solves an open question about generative vs. single-step performance. However, this exact experiment was already conducted and published in:

Lemercier, J. M., Richter, J., Welker, S., & Gerkmann, T. (2023, June). Analysing diffusion-based generative approaches versus discriminative approaches for speech restoration. In ICASSP 2023-2023 IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing (ICASSP) (pp. 1-5). IEEE.

Lemercier et al. showed that a discriminatively trained NCSN++ performs competitively with (and beats in some metrics like SI-SDR) diffusion generative models on speech enhancement tasks. The paper fails to cite or discuss this work, invalidating its claims of novelty regarding single-step NCSN++ performance.


3. Lack of Justification for the Contribution:
Discriminative models are already well known to perform strongly on VoiceBank-DEMAND. Matching a generative baseline on this dataset with a discriminative setup is unsurprising. The authors need to explain what this specific parameterization actually contributes over a standard discriminative baseline beyond simply reusing FlowSE's codebase.
